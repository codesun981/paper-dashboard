Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress reaffirmed the company’s commitment to open-source AI during Tuesday’s earnings call with analysts — the first since CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation.

“At Nvidia, we are committed to fostering a vibrant open model ecosystem, so every country, company, and industry can build its own intelligence,” Kress said in her opening remarks. She also noted that open models were accelerating AI adoption, citing a 35-fold year-over-year surge in weekly token volume on OpenRouter, including a 3.5-fold increase over the past three months.

Kress also spoke about Nvidia’s plans for Hugging Face, a platform for open-source AI models that the company agreed to acquire for $12.9 billion in September, in what would be the second-largest acquisition in Nvidia’s history.

The platform, which serves more than 118 million developers and 200,000 companies, is still on track to join Nvidia, despite Huang’s ouster. Huang had heavily advocated for open-source AI technology. His departure had raised questions about the company’s future approach to open-source AI models, with Hugging Face’s CEO called Huang’s departure “a sad day,” and promised to “keep fighting for open-source AI, harder than ever.”

So far, Nvidia’s plans for Hugging Face appear unchanged. The acquisition was not directly addressed during the Q&A portion of the earnings call.

“Together, we will scale its platform and make intelligence more open, more capable, and more accessible,” said Kress of Nvidia’s plans for Hugging Face.

Last week, Nvidia founder Huang resigned from his position as CEO following news about his extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based company Megaspeed who is suspected of being a Chinese spy. He’s also accused of having extramarital affairs with two direct reports and is now set to testify before Congress by Thanksgiving. Meanwhile, the SEC is investigating Nvidia over whether the company’s disclosures misled investors, The WSJ reported.

Within Nvidia, Huang pushed for the development of the Nemotron family of open-weight AI models built for long-running agentic AI systems and reasoning workloads. On today’s call, Kress noted that Nemotron is now the “most widely used U.S. open model on OpenRouter,” and it’s “where developers start to build their own intelligence.”

“That foundation is empowering the ecosystem,” she said.
