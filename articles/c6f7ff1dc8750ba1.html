The changes have alarmed publishers and others with business accounts.

Meta has tweaked its terms of service for business accounts after a swift backlash against an update scheduled to take effect later this month. The company removed a reference to AI training from its commercial terms, but there are still questions about its ability to scrape the websites, including those of publishers, that have business accounts on the platform.

The social media company initially shared a planned update to its commercial terms last week. As flagged by Nieman Lab, the new language stated that Meta would have the right to use web scrapers and other automated tools "to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs (and any content available through them)" associated with business accounts on the platform. "Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of, the Meta Company Products, services, and features," the terms said.

That language, which was going to take effect October 30, raised alarm among publishers and other businesses. The concern, as Nieman Lab explained, was that these terms would seem to give Meta the right to scrape the content of any website that also has a business account on Facebook or Instagram, a group that includes the vast majority of digital publishers.Then Meta quietly updated its commercial terms again. It removed the word "train" and added that scraping would be used "in order to provide, develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products."

Here's what that section now says in full [emphasis added]:

"You authorize us (or any entity acting on our behalf) to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents) and create test credentials to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs (and any content available through them) that are associated with your products or services, or that are made available through your or users' use of Meta Company Products and services, and to periodically pull updates from such domains, web pages, or URLs in order to provide, develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products (including business, commercial and related products to help you create and deliver ads and other commercial content, provide users information about your products and services, and connect you with users who may be interested in your products and services) and maintain the integrity and security of Meta Company Products."

In a statement, Meta spokesperson Andy Stone said the company had "clarified" the language to reflect that the new terms apply "only to our ads and business products." He said the changes "were not about scraping news sites" and that the company wasn't changing how it uses web crawlers.

But Stone's statement doesn't address the fact that the new terms are still "a substantial expansion of Meta's scraping permissions," as Nieman Lab reports in its latest piece on the changes. A number of legal experts told the publication that Meta is permitted to scrape news websites with seemingly few restrictions under the revised terms. Though Stone claims the changes "don't give us new rights to supersede" content owners' robots.txt preferences, robots.txt is not legally binding. And AI companies regularly evade robots.txt instructions.

It likely also doesn't help that Meta has had a tumultuous relationship with news publishers. The company stopped paying for news in 2022 and killed its dedicated news tab in 2024. More recently, the company has cut a handful of deals with outlets to provide news content for its AI chatbot.

Now, media groups are sounding the alarm about Meta's new terms. Danielle Coffey, CEO of the News/Media Alliance, said in a statement that the rules "are outrageous and probably illegal." Theo Bamber, the top executive of the UK's News Media Association, told Press Gazette it was a "shameless landgrab attempt" from the company.
